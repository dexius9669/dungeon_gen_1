Tricks and Traps
---------------
There are any number of tricks and traps for the unwary in the underworld.
Their purpose varies from simple amusement for the participants to fatality.
The Referee is encouraged to add to this list continually so that
participants never become bored or complacent.

Acid Bath - This is a pit or entire chamber filled with a highly corrosive
substance that may give off poisonous fumes. Typically, a means to cross to
the other side of the acid bath is provided, though it may be a rickety
ladder, dodgy rope, or other risky means. A character's skill or just blind
luck often determines who lives or dies. Armor may provide a few rounds of
protection against the acid.

Cage - When triggered, a cage falls on one or more characters, trapping them
for 1d6 turns before they can escape. If the cage is in a room, a randomly
determined treasure is often the bait to entice adventurers into the trap,
or to reward them if it is circumvented.

Chute - A panel in the floor gives way and the adventurer who triggered the
trap is swept downward to a lower level of the dungeon. Often, the chute has
one or more mechanisms to prevent someone from climbing back up such as
grease, a series of one way panels, retracting spikes (like in a modern
garage), etc.

Elevator Room - After a certain number of participants enter the room, the
doors seal and the room descends 1d3 levels. It will eventually return to its
original location, though not if occupied.

Man Eating Sea Weed - An entire corridor, cavern, or chamber is filled (or
starts filling) with an enormous quantity of Man Eating Sea Weed (150 HTK).
Sometimes the monster is located at the bottom of a chute, pit, or elevator.
Other times, an adventurer might trigger a sluice gate that allows the
corrosive substance to pour into the area.

Magical Portal - Those who pass through the portal are transported from one
location to another, however, the cosmic balance must be maintained.
Therefore, the HTK of any creature(s) sent through the portal are tallied
and within 1d6 turns one or more creatures (at random) emerge from the
portal until the HTKs are the same on both sides.

Pile of Bones - This is a deconstructed skeleton which, if reassembled, can do
one of four things:
  1. Attack the builder
  2. Serve the builder until destroyed
  3. Lead the builder to a randomly determined treasure
  4. Lead the builder to its master - a high level Magic-User capable of
     animating the dead

Pit - This falling trap may be open, concealed, or covered and triggered by
the weight of an adventurer. The fall will do some number of points of
damage to the character when he hits the ground - as much as 1d6 per ten
feet. A saving throw is sometimes provided to halve the damage. Typically, a
pit has no exit at the bottom.

Pit, Staked - As above except the bottom is lined with stakes that are likely
(4-in-6 chance) poisoned. If poisoned, a saving throw must be made to avoid
death. The stakes themselves do 1d6 points of damage in excess of the fall.

Pit, Fire - As above, however the character lands inside of an oven, forge, or
furnace. The shaft that the character fell down is the source from which the
fire draws fresh air. Another shaft nearby is the chimney and may lead
elsewhere in the dungeon or else to the surface. The character takes
additional damage (1d6 or 2d6) each round he remains in the fire.

Slide - An adventurer falls through a trapdoor and onto a slide which descends
to the next level of the dungeon. If the slide is in a room, the Referee
should roll up a random treasure which is used to bait the trap. The Referee
should have the player pick a number. This number determines into which
chamber on the next level the slide dumps him out. If there is a monster in
that room, the character must fight it (i.e. there is no chance of surprise
and the monster automatically has a negative reaction).

Sliding Panel - When one of the adventurers passes underneath, a panel will
suddenly slid down into place - blocking retreat. The party may make one
attempt to open it with a 1-in-6 chance that it slides back up. The panel can
be made of metal, stone, or wood. If made of metal, it cannot be circumvented
without several hours of work.

Trap Door - the weight of one or more adventurers causes it to give way,
dropping him or them to the next level of the dungeon.

Underworld Sea - The adventurers discover an entryway to a massive underworld
sea. Unless the adventurers have a way to construct a raft, there is no way to
explore further. The Referee might provide a pier and ship if such a
fantastic adventure is appealing. Or perhaps it is just an illusion?

Living Statue - This is an animated clay, stone, or metal statue. All can move,
while some are imbued with a limited ability to speak a message or have a
brief interaction. There is seemingly no limit to the size of the statue but
most are larger than a man yet smaller than a giant. Some are armed, others
use their arms and hands to crush or smash an opponent. A Living Statue is
usually bound to follow a set of instructions; most are used to guard a
location or treasure trove. A handful are quite beautiful like the one
sculpted by Pygmalion.

Haunting Spirit - A spirit that is bound to a particular location. The location
can be a single room, dwelling, graveyard, hilltop, manse, or an entire marsh.
The spirit makes its presence known by haunting. The spirit need not be that
of a human, it can also be that of a beloved pet such as a dog, cat, or other
animal. A Haunting Spirit is seldom seen, except out of the corner of the
eye, or moving in the shadows. Those who are sensitive to psychic energy (such
as a Magic-User or Cleric) may sense the spirit first. Encounters with a
Haunting Spirit are often just frightening though a few prefer to play
practical jokes. Sometimes they move or throw small objects, scream, recreate
a violent scene, foretell a doom, etc. However, there are a handful of
Haunting Spirits who act out physically. The latter type can draw blood by
clawing at a victim, push or trip someone, or even lift the person off the
ground and carry them about. Such attacks do 1d3 HTK points of damage. The
worst of these can cause death or insanity, especially during a stormy night
when their powers are at the peak. Such attacks do 1d6 HTK of damage but the
victim gets a saving throw to avoid harm. The details of any insanity are left
to the Referee.

Minotaur - This flesh-eating beast has a bovine head atop the powerful body of
a man or woman. A Minotaur is bad-tempered, not too bright, and as strong as
a bull. It does twice damage when it strikes. A Minotaur does not check
morale. It will automatically attack and pursue so long as its prey is in
sight.


UNDERWORLD ADVENTURES
---------------------
Referee uses graph paper and a pencil to lay out a map of a mine,
counter-mine, or dungeon. Many of these passages should not only run in each
of the cardinal directions, but also at 45 degree angles. In some places the
Referee notes on his map the position of connections to other levels of the
dungeon, traps, the presence of monsters (and treasure!), and regular and
secret doors.

During play, the Referee calls out distances and describes the rooms,
corridors, doors, etc. in reaction to the players' actions.

Wise players will make their own map of the underworld, or else find some way
to mark their progress so they can return to the surface.

Surprise
---------
The party has a 1-in-6 chance of being jumped by the baddies when they are
making noise or otherwise distracted (such as when a Cleric is healing a party
member). Monsters are subject to surprise as well.

Secret Doors
------------
Elves have a 4-in-6 chance of finding and opening a secret door. Other
characters have a 2-in-6 chance of merely locating a secret door. Secret doors
are often found at apparent dead ends.

Torches and Light
-----------------
Each torch lasts one hour. To make it easier to track, the Referee can simply
assume that time passes in the dungeon just as it does in real life. Using an
actual hourglass can help achieve the right atmosphere at the table. When the
time expires, the torch sputters out, no matter what is happening. This is
particularly unfortunate for adventurers who are in combat or running away
from monsters.

A torch does not emit much light, leaves behind smoke and soot, and otherwise
exposes adventurers to discovery. Because torchlight is so irregular, any
underground encounter takes place at 1d6 x 10 feet.

Stuck or Locked Doors
---------------------
Many doors in a dungeon are stuck or locked; a character has a 1-in-6 chance
of opening these. A rare few are both stuck and locked (1-in-12 chance of
opening). If the door won't budge, it can be kicked down or busted in (often
using an axe). This creates a lot of noise and requires an additional
wandering monster check.

Underworld Oddities
-------------------
Sometimes a room or corridor will contain (or lead to) a secret door,
stairway, special monster, special room, trick, or trap. These oddities are
in addition to any wandering monsters or keyed encounters. The oddities
immediately below are suggestive, not comprehensive. The Referee should add
his own oddities to keep things fresh. For additional guidance, see the
procedure for generating oddities in BOOKLET 4: RANDOM DUNGEON GENERATION.

Special Monsters
----------------
The Referee may introduce some monsters which pose a special challenge to
players. These monsters possess rare skills, weapons, or armament. Whatever
combinations the Referee devises, he should be careful not to introduce a
monster so powerful as to destroy play balance. Some examples follow:

• An Anti-Hero who is capable of high-level wizardry and wields an
  intelligent, magic sword.
• A Bealuwearg wearing an adamantine hauberk presents a deadly challenge to
  those who wish to fight.
• An insomniac Dragon or a Dragon with a poisonous bite in addition to its
  fiery breath.
• An Earth Elemental in the form of a gigantic Roc will attack any who comes
  near. It has 8 times the strength of a normal Roc and does octuple damage
  with every blow.
• A Ghoul with a magical shield strapped to its arm roams the dungeon.
• A Giant that breathes fire.
• A Troll, with armor made of a Cobalt Dragon's hide, has a magic sword. He
  searches for a worthy opponent to duel.

Special Rooms
-------------
Casino - Players that enter this room are teleported to a Las Vegas style room
where they have an opportunity to play games of chance against a Sage such as
a board game, card game, or dice game. Mundane loaded dice, as well as the
magical Undetectable Stacked Deck and Undetectable Loaded Dice are of use in
this situation. The Sage might offer any odds, stakes, and winnings the
Referee desires. Losers might sacrifice gold, HTK, nothing at all, etc. For
example, the Sage might offer to play a game Yahtzee[15] and grant the winner
25 experience points with no penalty for losing.

If "busted," a Sage may answer a question concerning his area of
specialization. Sages specialize in questions about living things, the
supernatural, or the physical world (Alchemy, Astrology, Physics, etc.). Very
difficult questions cannot be attempted in such a setting since the Sage is
obviously vacationing at the Casino and cannot consult his library.

A "busted" Sage can also be hired for either 20d10 gp per year, or else can be
hired to answer a single very difficult question for a flat rate of 500 gp.
If hired at the annual rate and dismissed from service without just reason,
however, the hiring character may never hire another Sage.

Designer Note: In FFC, a Scholar can be hired for the former rate, while in
Arneson v. Gygax, the latter fee is shown. Both Arneson and Gygax developed
intricate rules for Sages — Arneson in the Blackmoor™ supplement and Gygax in
the Dungeon Masters Guide for AD&D 1st edition. That book has an entire
appendix dedicated to the rules for dice and card games. Per Jon Peterson's
commentary in the Rules to the Game of Dungeon, craps and Yahtzee were
definitely played by gamers in the Twin Cities.

Anyone who attacks a Sage is automatically changed to Chaotic alignment unless
the Sage himself was Chaotic (1-in-3 chance). When a Sage is about to die from
a murderous assault, the Sage is able to cast a curse on the person or
creature that reduced him to zero HTK. There is a 1-in-4 chance that the Sage
was sufficiently knowledgable that this curse can have a devastating effect
such as preventing the cursed slayer from ever making a saving throw.
Otherwise, the curse of lesser effect such as having all of the murderer's
hair fall out. The only means to remove such a curse is to complete a
religious Quest for a Cleric or Patriarch.

Movie Theater - Just as with the Casino, this room provides a temporary escape
to the "real world" for the participants in the game. Most likely, this was
used as a break to watch television, especially if a sword and sandal movie or
horror flick was programmed.
